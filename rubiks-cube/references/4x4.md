# 4x4 reference (reduction method)

Idea: make the 4x4 look like a 3x3, then solve it as a 3x3 with `3x3.md`.

1. **Centers**: build all six 2x2 centers.
2. **Edges**: pair the two wings of each edge into a "dedge". 12 dedges.
3. **Solve as a 3x3**, using only outer moves (`R U F ...`) and wide moves of
   whole groups. Two extra problems can appear here: **OLL parity** and **PLL parity**.

All algs here are checked by `scripts/test_cube.py`. Write moves as `2R` (inner
slice only) and `Rw` (two layers); never lowercase (see `notation.md`).

## 1. Centers

Intuitive. Main tricks:

- Build the first center (e.g. white on U or D) with slice moves like `2R U 2R'`.
- Build the opposite center next. Remember colour order: on a standard cube
  white-yellow, blue-green, red-orange are opposite; with white on U and green on
  F, red is on R (check the corners of the user's cube if unsure).
- Last four centers: keep solved centers on U and D, bring pieces with
  `Rw U Rw'`, `Rw U2 Rw'` style moves, restoring the solved centers each time.
- Last two centers: move 1x2 bars between them with a wide move, a U2, and the
  wide move back (e.g. `Rw U2 Rw'`), restoring the other centers.

## 2. Edge pairing

Pair edges one by one with slice moves (`2R`, `Uw`, ...), then fix centers back.
The standard tool is **slice-flip-slice**, which is also how you do the last two edges.

Put the two unpaired edges at **FL** and **FR** (front-left and front-right,
middle layer). In each slot one wing is a "stranger" that belongs to the other
slot. Find where the strangers are and pick:

| Strangers at | Alg |
|---|---|
| FL top, FR bottom | `Uw' R U R' F R' F' R Uw` |
| FL bottom, FR top | `Dw R F' U R' F Dw'` |
| both top | `R U R' F R' F' R`, then `Uw' R U R' F R' F' R Uw` |
| both bottom | `R U R' F R' F' R`, then `Dw R F' U R' F Dw'` |

(`R U R' F R' F' R` flips the FR edge in place, which moves its stranger to the
other height.) All four keep centers and keep other paired edges paired; the top
layer and some corners get mixed, which does not matter at this stage.

### "One last edge looks wrong" during pairing

If eleven dedges are paired, the twelfth **is** paired too: its two wings must
belong together. A wing cannot flip in its slot, so the only way the last edge
can look wrong is that **both wings are swapped = the whole dedge is flipped**.
That is OLL parity (below). Same fix whether you notice it now or during 3x3 OLL.

## 3. Parity (after reduction)

### OLL parity: one edge flipped

Symptom: during 3x3 OLL you have an odd number of flipped edges (1 or 3), which
is impossible on a 3x3.

Hold the flipped edge at **UF**, U face on top.

**Pure (changes only the UF edge, nothing else):**

`2R2 B2 U2 2L U2 2R' U2 2R U2 F2 2R F2 2L' B2 2R2`

Verified: exactly 4 stickers change (the two wings of UF swap = edge flips).
Centers, corners and all other edges stay. Use this when the cube is otherwise
solved or nearly solved. Another pure one (Lucas parity):

`2R' U2 2L F2 2L' F2 2R2 U2 2R U2 2R' U2 F2 2R2 F2`

**Speed version (common in tutorials):**

`Rw U2 x Rw U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Lw U2 Rw' U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Rw'`

Flips UF but **also** swaps UFL↔UFR corners and UL↔UR edges. Only use it before
3x3 OLL/PLL are done (it keeps the first two layers and all centers). It is
easier to remember and fast with wide moves.

### PLL parity: two edges swapped

Symptom: at PLL, two edges look swapped (or a case not in the PLL list, like two
edges plus nothing else, or a single diagonal-looking swap).

Swap UF and UB (only those two edges, nothing else):

`2R2 U2 2R2 Uw2 2R2 2U2`

(Same as `2R2 U2 2R2 Uw2 2R2 Uw2` followed by `U2`.) For other swaps: do this,
then finish with a normal 3x3 PLL.

### Both parities

Fix OLL parity first (flip the edge), then solve 3x3 OLL, then PLL; PLL parity
may or may not appear.

## Checking a move sequence

```
python3 ~/.claude/skills/rubiks-cube/scripts/cube.py apply -n 4 "ALG"   # what ALG does to a solved cube
python3 ~/.claude/skills/rubiks-cube/scripts/cube.py case  -n 4 "ALG"   # what state ALG solves
```

The report shows wrong stickers, whether centers are solved, which edge slots
are unpaired, and which pieces visibly moved.
